# Canary v1.3 — release audit

**Revision audited:** branch `codex/v13-just-works`, HEAD `768cbe1` — **30 commits** on top of `main`
(`b7e2cae`), numbered slices 1-29 plus a `16b` correction. `main` is untouched and nothing has been pushed.
**What this document is:** an evidence review of the six objective parts and every trust invariant, with the
blockers that remain.
**What it is NOT:** a release. Nothing is merged to `main`, pushed, tagged or published, and this document
does not assert that v1.3 is ready to ship — it states what is proven, what is not, and what still needs a
decision.

Every number below is quoted from an executed reporter. Where a claim is not measured, it says so.

## 3. The token position, stated at its measured size

**What v1.3 may claim**

- The **everyday shape** costs **92.7 %** of a plain agent on the three-fixture arm map, with equal
  correctness (12/12, 15/15, 406/406) and **no false done in any arm** — i.e. it removes almost no tokens and
  adds an independent completion check.

  **Per cell, because one aggregate hides the shape of it** (n = 1 per cell, read from the records):

  | fixture | plain | everyday | ratio |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | bound-requirements | 126,977 | 105,255 | **82.9 %** (−17.1 %) |
  | bug-sum | 70,070 | 70,083 | **100.02 %** (parity) |
  | stateful-replay | 212,777 | 204,454 | **96.1 %** (−3.9 %) |
  | **total** | **409,824** | **379,792** | **92.7 %** |

  So the everyday saving is **not uniform**: one cell is 17 % cheaper, one is exact parity, one is 4 %
  cheaper. The aggregate is a sum of those three, and the honest description of the everyday shape is
  *"parity or slightly better, never worse"* — not a uniform discount.
- The **long stateful task does not explode** on the everyday shape: **17 turns, 96.1 % of plain**.
- The **confined transport** is dramatically cheaper on short, bound, well-specified work — and that claim is
  now replicated **three times on each side**, not just the canary side:

  | cell | plain (3 runs) | canary (3 runs) | ratio range | every pairing ≤75 %? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | bound-requirements | 126,977 · 135,531 · 157,104 | 46,336 · 20,562 · 30,188 | **13.1-36.5 %** | **yes** |
  | bug-sum | 70,070 · 69,287 · 69,634 | 50,787 · 37,868 · 39,744 | **54.0-73.3 %** | **yes** |

  `deliveredCorrect` was `true` in all six canary runs. The widest pairing in either cell is **73.3 %**, so
  **the original −25 % target is achieved — replicated, on both arms — on 2 of the 3 cells confined mode can
  execute.** Note that the plain baseline is not a moving target: bug-sum's plain is almost constant
  (69,287-70,070), while bound-requirements' plain is *higher* on replication than the single original run
  (mean 139,871 vs 126,977), which if anything makes the canary ratio more favourable rather than less.

- It is **more expensive on the long stateful cell** (120-260 %), which dominates the aggregate, so the
  aggregate stays above target: **86.8 % by median, 110.5 % by mean**, using the paired distributions above
  and the long cell's own replicates. The ≤75 % target is met per-cell and not in aggregate, and both numbers
  are stated because one hides the other.

- **`CANARY_CONFINED_CHECK` does NOT generalise, and that is now measured rather than suspected.** The flag
  was isolated on both short cells by running each again with it turned off (`v13noflag*`):

  | cell | with flag (mean) | without flag (mean) | effect |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | bound-requirements | **32,362 — 25.5 %** | 51,050 — 40.2 % | helps (~37 % lower) |
  | bug-sum | **42,800 — 61.1 %** | 25,999 — 37.1 % | **hurts (~65 % higher)** |

  The two cells move in **opposite directions**, and on bug-sum the no-flag pair (24,428 · 27,569) sits
  entirely below the with-flag triple (50,787 · 37,868 · 39,744). The flag therefore cannot be described as
  an improvement; it is a change whose sign depends on the task. **It stays opt-in**, which is no longer a
  caution about n = 1 but a measured conclusion — and it is a good example of why the release does not flip a
  default on the strength of the one cell where the effect looked large and favourable.

- **A methodological caveat, printed by the harness itself and recorded rather than buried.** All three
  fixtures declare `benchmarkConfig: {"arm":"guarded", ...}`, so `plain` and confined runs are — in
  `run-trial.mjs`'s own words — *"an experiment outside the configuration the fixture was authored and
  validated for; do not present it as that fixture's measurement."* This applies **equally to both arms** of
  the confined comparison, so the ratios remain like-for-like (same fixture bytes, task, model and hidden
  oracle), but no absolute figure here should be quoted as a fixture's validated measurement. **The everyday
  numbers are the exception and the stronger case: `guarded` *is* the authored configuration**, so the 92.7 %
  above rests on the arm those fixtures were designed and validated for.

**What v1.3 may NOT claim**

- **Not ≤75 % in aggregate.** No configuration reached the target across all three cells. It **is** reached,
  replicated three times, on the two cells below — what fails is the aggregate, and the long cell is the
  whole of the reason. This is the one objective part that is not met, and the distinction between "not met
  anywhere" and "met on 2 of 3 cells but not in aggregate" is the difference between a failure and a
  measured scope.
- **Not "the everyday path isolates or promotes."** It does not: there is no candidate and no frozen task
  authority. The *guarantee* that lifecycle exists to provide is measured present by other means
  (`v13-verified-bytes.mjs`), and building the machinery was rejected with reasons (a second gate
  implementation; ~2× the everyday token cost).
- **Not seamless on any harness but Claude Code**, and not at all on Cursor, which is unmeasured.
- **Not `HARDENED`** on any host without a measured deployment.
- **Not a demonstrated correctness advantage.** The v1.2 corpus already had the plain model at ceiling
  (36/36 delivered correct); v1.3's value there is the completion guarantee, not a higher pass rate.

**The measured cause, investigated once (the brief asks for exactly one such pass, and this is it).** The
question is where the confined long cell's remaining excess sits. Decomposed from the recorded per-run
figures — arithmetic on measurements, not a new measurement:

| | pre-check (v13edit) | with check (v13check) | plain |
|---|---|---|---|
| total | 595,419 | 288,942 | 212,777 |
| cache-read per turn | 21,587 (**2.14×** plain) | 12,298 (**1.22×** plain) | 10,091 |
| turns | 24 | 19 | 18 |

The per-batch check did not make the worker do less work; it cut the **context it carried per turn** from
2.14× plain to 1.22×, by removing the round trips that asked for information the tool result could carry.
The residual excess on that run (+76,165, +35.8 %) decomposes roughly as **+42 K from the still-larger
per-turn context, +15 K from more output, +12 K from one extra turn, +8 K from cache-create** — no single
dominant term, and the three replicated runs bracket it (the best, 255,756, is **1.20×** plain; the median is
1.36×).

**No low-complexity lever remains.** The residual is the structural cost of routing every operation through
one tool in one long-lived context instead of using a native agent's tools, and the only way found to reduce
it is the per-batch check that is already built. Per the brief, this is the one investigation; there is no
second speculative loop to enter, and the ≤75 % target is not to be chased by weakening verification.

**Replication is why the numbers above are trustworthy — and why one earlier claim was withdrawn.** Both
sides of the dominant cell now have replicates:

| long cell | runs | mean | median | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain | 212,777 · 148,286 · 208,252 | 189,772 | 208,252 | 1.44× |
| confined + per-batch check | 288,942 · 553,036 · 255,756 | 365,911 | 288,942 | 2.16× |
| confined, no check | 585,337 · 595,419 | 590,378 | 590,378 | 1.02× |

## 5. Verdict

**The code is in a releasable state; the *claim* is not yet decided.**

- All six objective parts are delivered except the ≤75 % target, and **the target itself is now partly met**:
  it is achieved — replicated three times on each arm — on the two short bound cells that confined mode can
  execute (13.1-36.5 % and 54.0-73.3 %), and missed only in aggregate, where the long stateful cell dominates
  (86.8 % by median, 110.5 % by mean). The everyday shape sits at parity or slightly better and is the
  fixtures' **authored** arm. Nothing was weakened to get any of this, and no further optimisation is planned.
- Every v1.2 trust invariant survives, measured, at 94 PASS / 2 host-bound SKIP.
- The docs describe the shipped product and now fail the build if either of the two known overclaims
  reappears (that `plain` was wired; that the 75 % target was met).

**Safe to begin the final v1.3 release audit: YES** — this document is that audit, and the condition it
carried (the re-scope being recorded rather than dropped) is met in `README.md` and in §3 above.

**Not done, and not to be done without the owner's word:** merging to `main`, pushing, tagging, publishing,
flipping `CANARY_CONFINED_CHECK` on by default, or pursuing further token optimisation to cross 75 %.
